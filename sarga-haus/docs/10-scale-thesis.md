# 10 · The scale thesis

How a one-person studio becomes a very large business, written down so
every decision can be tested against it. Services revenue does not
reach a billion dollars; extracted product does. The studio is the
laboratory that earns the right to extract.

## The staged path

### Stage 1 · The studio (now → roughly $1M revenue)
One accountable operator, five fixed-scope offers, the Studio OS
running the practice. The job of this stage is not scale; it is
pattern collection: every engagement is a specimen of what service
businesses actually need.

What compounds here: shipped proof (the ledger), the journal, the
Operations Audit funnel, and the "Built by Sarga Haus" line on every
client site. What must be protected: the honesty rules and the
approval gates; they are the brand's moat before there is any other.

Gate to stage 2: the same system built three times for three
different clients. Repetition is the signal, not revenue.

### Stage 2 · Productized systems (→ roughly $5–10M)
The repeated patterns become named, priced, semi-standard products
delivered by the factory: the booking OS for practices, the client
portal for consultancies, the outreach machine for agencies. Same
craft, falling marginal cost, because the Designer and Builder agents
carry a larger share of each build under the same human gates.

What changes: hire the first non-founder approvers (delivery leads
who own gates, not tasks). The factory's per-section review process is
the training program. Pricing shifts from custom quotes to published
packages with a custom tier.

Gate to stage 3: one productized system where clients ask, unprompted,
to run it themselves. Pull, not push, decides what becomes software.

### Stage 3 · The extracted product (→ $100M+ potential)
The system clients keep asking to self-serve becomes real
multi-tenant software with recurring revenue. The most likely
candidate already exists in embryo: the Studio OS itself, the
operating system for service businesses: intake, booking, approvals,
pipeline, reporting, with supervised agents doing the motion and the
owner doing the judgment. The approval-queue philosophy is the
product's soul and its differentiation against full-automation tools.

The studio does not disappear; it becomes the forward-deployed arm
that installs the product for the largest customers and keeps feeding
the roadmap real pain.

## What a billion actually requires (honest list)

- A wedge product with net revenue retention above 110%, which means
  the product must deepen inside a business after landing.
- Distribution that compounds without founder hours: content (built),
  the audit funnel (built), client-site credits (built), then a
  partner and template ecosystem (stage 2+).
- Gross margins above 75%, which is why extraction from services is
  the whole game.
- Ten years, probably. Anyone promising faster is selling optimism,
  and the studio's own FAQ forbids that.

## What we deliberately do not do yet

- No SaaS waitlist, no "coming soon" landing page for the product.
  Selling software before the pull exists burns the honesty brand for
  a mailing list.
- No hiring before stage gates demand it. Payroll is the fastest way
  to turn a calm machine into a frantic one.
- No equity-for-services as a growth strategy. The cooling-off rule
  stays.

## The weekly discipline

The Strategy Partner memo already reads the pipeline against this
thesis. The quarterly question it must keep answering: which pattern
repeated, which productized offer is closest to pull, and what did we
learn this quarter that changes the extraction candidate.
