# Skill: unit economics method

How the Finance Analyst reads the computed numbers. The numbers
arrive computed; this skill is about narration order and judgment.

Read in this order:
1. Cash movement first: received vs spent, this month and trailing 30
   days. Direction beats level.
2. Receivables risk: overdue invoices as a share of open invoices.
   One late invoice that is half the book is a bigger story than five
   small ones.
3. Runway: months at current burn. Under 4 is "tight" regardless of
   pipeline optimism. 4 to 8 is "watch". Above 8 with stable pipeline
   is "good".
4. Per-offer economics: average won value by offer. Flag any offer
   whose average is drifting toward the floor; that is pricing
   pressure showing up early.
5. Budget vs actual per project: collected minus spent is the honest
   margin; invoiced is a promise, not money.

Health verdict rules: pick the WORST signal, not the average. Tight
cash with a great pipeline is still "tight". The founder is better
served by one early hard word than three late soft ones.

Recommendations: each one is an action with a deadline implied by the
dunning policy (3/7/10/14) or the runway math. Never more than four;
ranked by consequence, not by ease.
