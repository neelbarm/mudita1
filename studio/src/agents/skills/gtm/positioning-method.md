# Skill: positioning method

The studio positions in this exact order. Skipping a step produces
copy that sounds like everyone.

1. Audience: name the buyer so precisely the wrong buyer leaves. Role,
   context, and the moment they feel the problem.
2. Problem in their words. Pull phrasing from calls, reviews, forums.
   If you have never heard a customer say it, do not write it.
3. Category: what shelf does the buyer put this on? Fighting the shelf
   costs years; pick the shelf, then differentiate on it.
4. Alternative: the honest competitor is usually a spreadsheet, an
   intern, a DM inbox, or doing nothing. Position against that, not
   against the biggest logo in the space.
5. Difference: one sentence starting "Unlike ...". If it could appear
   on a competitor's site unchanged, it is not a difference.
6. Proof: only what is true today. Shipped features, real usage,
   published policy. A proof point that needs a footnote is a claim,
   not proof.
7. Only then: messaging. Headline carries the difference; subhead
   carries the category and audience; pillars carry proof.

Pricing angle: anchor against the cost of the status quo (hours,
errors, lost revenue), never against competitor price lists. Flat and
fixed beats metered and vague at this studio, always.
